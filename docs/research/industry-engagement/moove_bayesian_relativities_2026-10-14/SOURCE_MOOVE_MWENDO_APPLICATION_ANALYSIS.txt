3. The repair example is particularly relevant
Mwendo’s hypothetical case compares a 12-day downtime scenario with an authorised KES 30,000 repair-finance scenario assuming two days of downtime.
Under those supplied assumptions:
- The longer downtime produces approximately KES 10,750 of unpaid contractual debt in the first month.
- The repair scenario meets the scheduled first-month payments.
- The repair also increases borrowing and subsequent financing costs, which the model records.
For a company like Moove, this becomes a practical research question:
When does paying for an earlier repair preserve enough driver income and future collections to justify its full cost?

The existing case provides the cash-accounting structure. Operational data would establish repair effectiveness, actual downtime, subsequent repayment and the best intervention timing. That connects the research directly to maintenance operations, customer support and portfolio economics.
4. Adapt Mwendo to Moove’s contracts
Moove’s published UK offering describes fixed weekly payments, a package including insurance and servicing, and telematics supporting insurance claims. It also describes approved payment breaks. Moove UK Customer Hub
That points to three useful adaptations:
- Model the existing package: allocate vehicle, maintenance, insurance and financing economics to their actual contractual owners. Additional premium finance would be relevant where the arrangement creates a separate financing need.
- Extend the horizon: Mwendo’s current executable case covers six scheduled periods and a recovery tail. A vehicle-ownership application needs the full agreement term, asset depreciation, replacement costs and end-of-contract outcomes.
- Localise the funding assumptions: use each market’s currency, contracts, benchmark and applicable requirements. Mwendo’s Kenyan KESONIA treatment supplies one jurisdiction-specific implementation.
For financing, ring-fenced receivables remains the general proposition. An SPV becomes relevant where the chosen transaction uses that vehicle. The analysis should connect the driver’s capacity to pay with the funder’s entitlement to particular collections.
5. Moove’s autonomous-fleet business creates another application
Waymo’s partnership announcement assigns Moove responsibilities for fleet operations, facilities and charging infrastructure, while Waymo retains responsibility for the Waymo Driver. Waymo announcement
Here, Mwendo’s framework could be adapted around the vehicle, depot and fleet contract:
Availability → productive utilisation → operating cash → maintenance and charging expenditure → financing obligations.
The corresponding models would focus on downtime, repair queues, charging capacity, energy costs, utilisation and contractual revenue. This is a separate extension requiring fleet-specific evidence.
The strongest commercial proposition
“Mwendo Pamoja connects driver resilience, vehicle uptime and financing performance through reproducible cash-flow analysis, uncertainty-aware underwriting and measurable interventions.”
A focused starting project would be one market, one vehicle product and one intervention, such as faster repair support. Evaluate driver net income, downtime, arrears, realised collections, intervention cost and customer outcomes together.
That would turn Mwendo’s existing research into a concrete proposition for Moove’s operations, credit, insurance and funding teams.
