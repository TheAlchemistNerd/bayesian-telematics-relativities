2. Your paper explains how to turn that evidence into insurance quantities
The [Bayesian Relativities paper](C:/Users/Nevo/Downloads/bayesian-telematics-relativities/whitepapers/telematics-relativities/paper.md) separates:
- Exposure: how much insured driving occurs.
- Frequency: expected claims per unit of exposure.
- Severity: expected cost when a claim occurs.
- Non-driving losses: separately modelled risks such as theft or damage while parked.
In its simplified frequency–severity calculation:
Expected driving claims cost = exposure × claim rate per exposure unit × mean claim cost
That is useful for Moove because a highly productive driver may accumulate more kilometres while maintaining a relatively low claim rate per kilometre. The model can distinguish how much someone drives from the estimated risk associated with each unit of driving.

3. Bayesian credibility makes it usable for individual drivers
A new driver brings limited personal experience. Your hierarchy combines that experience with information from relevant fleet, territory and vehicle groups, progressively increasing the influence of the individual record as evidence accumulates.
The useful decision package includes:
- An estimated frequency or severity relativity.
- Its credible interval, showing parameter uncertainty.
- A predictive loss distribution, incorporating uncertainty in future claims.
- Exposure volume, claims maturity and data-quality indicators.
For a reviewer, this answers both “What does the evidence suggest?” and “How much confidence should we place in this individual estimate?”
The recursive Gamma–Poisson and inverse-Gamma–Gamma updates also support computational feasibility. Under the specified conditional models, individual experience can be updated through a small set of accumulated statistics, alongside scheduled recalibration of the wider model.

5. Profit testing is where the proposition becomes commercially persuasive
A useful Moove application would compare complete scenarios:
Existing arrangement → telematics-informed arrangement → intervention-and-incentive arrangement
For each scenario, track three perspectives:
- Insurer: premiums, claims, expenses, reinsurance and capital requirements.
- Moove: insurance payments, retained costs, downtime, servicing expenditure, collections and funding.
- Driver: total payments, excesses, incentives, days able to work and take-home cash.
Your paper’s tariff normalisation preserves an approved expected-loss level while reallocating relativities within the calibration population. Demonstrated reductions in claims would support a separate reassessment of that level and the benefits available to share.
The paper also draws on actuarial research examining how reinsurance and capital decisions affect the distribution of long-term shareholder returns—an appropriate foundation for this profit-testing extension. Robbin and Malhotra, CAS Forum
