Profit testing asks more than whether telematics can predict claims: does a programme improve the economics of insurance, fleet operations and driver work?

CGAP’s 2023 study, *Putting Gig Data to Work*, helped connect platform activity, vehicle finance and gig-worker outcomes. My new article develops the insurance extension for Moove and similar mobility businesses.

Bayesian Relativities separates exposure, claim frequency and severity. Hierarchical credibility supports sparse driver records with relevant group experience; posterior intervals show uncertainty. The predictive distribution informs pricing, reserves, reinsurance and capital.

The KES illustration uses a synthetic 100-vehicle portfolio, not Moove data. A flat KES 130,000 premium and a neutral stable/volatile tariff produce the same weighted premium. Neutral relativities redistribute expected loss; a measured intervention can change expected total loss. Under a stipulated, unobserved 10% frequency reduction for the volatile group, simulated mean insurer profit after capital moves from KES 1.307 million to KES 1.442 million. Predictive ranges and downside probabilities show the uncertainty. The article follows claim delays, treaty cash, capital, operator costs, repair downtime and driver incentives.

It follows three ledgers: insurer premiums, claims, expenses, reinsurance and capital; operator premium, retained loss, repairs, downtime and retention; driver earnings, weekly charges, incentives and cash. A separate Mwendo 156-week case maps annual insurance rates into weekly package cash and repair scenarios.
